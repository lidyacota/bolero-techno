About

Bolero Techno

A work of electronic literature from Mexico

Some books are read in silence; others ask for a dance floor. Bolero Techno belongs to the second kind. Its poems already carried music inside them (a sleepless bolero, a folk song of poverty, a ballad for Peter Pan, a dub that tumbles in the sea) and this project brought that music to the surface.

Here, each poem by Mexican poet Abraham Truxillo has a second body: a song. The idea of giving them that body, and of turning the book into a work of electronic literature, came from Mexican digital creator Lidya Cota, who also carried it out. Twenty-seven texts from the book became pieces she produced with artificial intelligence tools, ranging across bolero and mambo, salsa and ranchera, punk, ska, dub, corridos tumbados, slam poetry, the folk music of the Isthmus of Tehuantepec, and electronic music. The poem is never lost in the rhythm: you listen to it and, just below, you read it in full, in its original Spanish, with the spacing and indentation the author gave it on the page.

What happens on this site is electronic literature in a very concrete sense: poetry leaves the printed book, passes through artificial intelligence, circulates on digital platforms, and asks again questions that tradition considered settled: who the author of a work is, what language is made of, and how a poem reaches its readers today… and its listeners. Bolero Techno does not replace the book; it expands it. It takes it out dancing.

The project

Poetry, music, and technology

Bolero Techno is a work of electronic literature by Lidya Cota based on the poems of the book of the same name by Abraham Truxillo (Manofalsa, Lima, 2025): an artistic, literary, musical, and digital project of AI-assisted poetry musicalization. Lidya Cota conceived the project and, starting from the texts of the book, produced original sound compositions with artificial intelligence tools, in which the poetic voice intertwines with musical landscapes.

The work blends literature, electronic music, retro aesthetics, and contemporary sensibility. It explores the hybridity of formats and languages through experimentation and emotion, and the relationship between literature, technology, and feeling through the fusion of word and sound, in search of new forms of poetic expression in the digital environment.

The process was one of attentive listening. Lidya Cota chose the poems and, for each one, intuitively defined the genre, rhythm, arrangement, mix, and type of voice, and turned those decisions into the instructions from which the music was generated. Those instructions appear on each poem's sound sheet, so the process stays visible alongside the work.

Why it matters

A bolero for the digital age

The bolero is the great romantic song of Latin America: born in Cuba and adopted by Mexico as its own, it has carried the region's love and heartbreak for more than a century. The book's title collides that tradition with techno, the music of machines. This project makes the collision literal: verses written by a poet from Acapulco become boleros, mambos, ska, punk, and dub through artificial intelligence, without losing a single word.

In the AI era, Bolero Techno proposes that the value of these tools lies in human direction. Every poem was chosen, and every sound decided, by a woman creator from Latin America, a region and a perspective still underrepresented in electronic literature. The result opens contemporary Mexican poetry to listeners who may never open a poetry book, and to readers anywhere in the world.

Since 2024, individual poems, mostly by classic authors, have been turned into songs with AI tools and shared online. As far as we have been able to document, Bolero Techno is one of the first works to combine four traits: it starts from an already published poetry book; it was made in collaboration with its living author; it was conceived from the start as electronic literature; and it was created in May 2025, shortly after the book's publication.

How it works

Each page, three layers

The home page is a constellation of pages floating in 3D over a spinning record: every page is a poem. Each poem then has its own page with the song, presented as a video; the full poem, transcribed from the book with its original spacing, indentation, and italics; and the sound sheet, with the genre, voice, and mood instructions used to generate the music.

The book Bolero Techno (Manofalsa, Lima, 2025) gathers poems and anti-poems that cross popular culture, literary tradition, and contemporary sensibility. It closes with a “Bonus track: 9 mashups”, centos that mix verses from the Spanish Golden Age with today's popular music.

Abraham Truxillo

  • Author of the poems
  • Author of the book Bolero Techno (Manofalsa, Lima, 2025)

Born in Acapulco, Guerrero, Mexico, in 1983. Poet, essayist, and fiction writer. He holds a degree in Hispanic Literature from the National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM). Author of Postales del ventrílocuo (Ediciones Sin Nombre, 2011), Huracán. Antipoema (Dogma, 2025), Bestiario marino (Medusa, 2025), and Bolero Techno (Manofalsa, 2025).

www.abrahamtruxillo.com

Lidya Cota and Abraham Truxillo are married and have created several works of electronic literature together, in which words find other bodies: animation, interaction, and music.

Recognition

El Aleph Festival 2026

Selected for UNAM's El Aleph Festival of Art and Science

El Aleph. Festival de Arte y Ciencia is the National Autonomous University of Mexico's (UNAM) festival devoted to the dialogue between the arts, the sciences, and the humanities. In 2026 it celebrated its tenth anniversary under the theme New Narratives, focused on interpreting the present in the face of artificial intelligence, the climate crisis, and disinformation, with more than 170 activities.

Bolero Techno was selected for this tenth edition in the Multidiscipline category, with the session “Bolero Techno y Bestiario marino: literatura electrónica”, led by Lidya Cota and Abraham Truxillo. It was presented as a work that goes beyond the traditional literary format to become an expanded art project, raising questions about authorship, the materiality of language, and new forms of artistic production.

Saturday, May 16, 2026 · 10:00 a.m. · Aldea Aleph (Cabinas científicas), Explanada de la Espiga, Ciudad Universitaria · Mexico City
